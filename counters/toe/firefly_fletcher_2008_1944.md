# Fletcher, *Sherman VC M4A4 Firefly* (Military Ordnance Special No. 19) — Direct Read

Full read (24 pages, image scan, no OCR text layer, unusually large/high-resolution page
scans) of David Fletcher, *Sherman VC M4A4 Firefly* (Darlington Productions, Military
Ordnance Special Number 19, copyright 1997), specifically to resolve the mantlet-AV
provenance question, the Firefly-specific APDS timing question, and cross-check the
three historical validation vignettes and other hull/turret figures already recorded in
`sherman_firefly_1944.md`.

**Important note on the book itself**: despite the cover date sometimes cited as "2008"
(a reprint/later-printing date), the copyright page in this scan reads **1997**, publisher
Darlington Productions, Inc., P.O. Box 5884, Darlington, Maryland. This is a photo-heavy
technical monograph (18 pages of running text plus extensive photo captions, then a large
photo-and-line-drawing section, pp.6-24) — not a combat-narrative unit history. That
distinction matters directly for Finding 3 below.

## Finding 1 (most important): the mantlet-AV "+13mm" claim is NOT in this book — at all

This was the single most important question this pass was dispatched to answer, and the
answer is a clean, confident negative. The book's entire discussion of the mantlet (p.3,
"The Turret" section) is purely descriptive/functional, with zero armor-thickness figures
for the mantlet casting itself:

> "The mantlet for the Firefly was not unlike the M34A1 mount fitted to regular Shermans,
> with the co-axial .30 caliber Browning machine gun on the left and the No. 43 sighting
> telescope on the right. The whole assembly was classified in the British system as
> 'Mounting, 17 Pr., & Browning .300in. M.G., No 2, Mk I.'"

That is the complete extent of the book's mantlet description in running text. The rest of
the mantlet-related content in this book is: two close-up photographs (p.15) of the
Bovington Firefly's mantlet showing casting texture and a spot weld/plug, captioned only
"Closeup of the left side of the Firefly mantlet" and "Looking down on the mantlet you can
see various casting impressions on the turret and hull" (no thickness callouts); two line
drawings labeled simply "MANTLET" and "Mantlet and shield drawings" showing the mantlet's
outline/profile with no dimensions printed on them; and a full page of turret/turret-ring
dimensional diagrams (p.16) giving *external* dimensions only — turret ring diameters
(56.0" inner / 69.0" outer), turret height figures (30.25", 38.5", 31.25"), and hull/vehicle
length and height figures — none of which is an armor-thickness figure for the mantlet
plate itself.

**This means Wikipedia's "+13mm" mantlet-thickness-delta claim, wherever it actually
originates, is not sourced to *this* Fletcher book's own text, despite this book being the
one most plausibly meant by a bare "Fletcher" citation for the Firefly specifically.** This
project's existing `sherman_firefly_1944.md` already flagged the claim as coming through a
Wikipedia footnote only, unconfirmed by direct reading — that flag stands, and is now
*strengthened*: the most obvious primary-source candidate has been read in full and does
not contain the claim. Two honest possibilities remain: (a) the real source is a different
Fletcher work (he wrote several Sherman/Firefly titles — e.g. Osprey New Vanguard 141,
*Sherman Firefly*, 2008, a full-length book distinct from this 1997 Special), or (b) the
claim traces to a non-Fletcher source and was mis-attributed somewhere upstream (a common
failure mode for Wikipedia armor figures, as this project has now observed repeatedly this
session). This project's current `vehicles.csv` Turret Front figure for the Firefly should
continue to carry its existing uncertainty flag — it is **not resolved by this pass**, only
narrowed (one specific candidate source ruled out).

## Finding 2: APDS timing — "summer of 1944," not a specific date, and explicitly qualified as poor-performing

Page 5 ("Firefly Performance") gives the book's only APDS-timing statement:

> "A report from 21st Army Group claimed that APC was effective against the frontal armor
> of a Tiger at 1,200 yards; APCBC at 2,000 yards and beyond, and APDS at well over 2,000
> yards. Unfortunately APDS, which was not available until the summer of 1944, proved to
> be wildly inaccurate at anything over 1,000 yards."

This directly addresses the open APDS-timing question in `sherman_firefly_1944.md` (which
flagged "March 1944 general 17pdr APDS date vs. unconfirmed 'August 1944' Firefly-specific
claim" as unresolved). **This book's own figure is "summer of 1944" — broader and vaguer
than the previously-flagged "August 1944" claim, but not inconsistent with it** (August
falls within "summer"). It does not confirm or deny the specific August date; it simply
gives a season-level figure from a different angle (general War Office/21st Army Group
ammunition-availability reporting, not a specific unit's first-use date). Also newly
notable: the book independently corroborates that **APDS accuracy was poor beyond 1,000
yards even after it became available** — a real operational caveat that this project's
existing rules treatment (Rule 17.3.1 band-reads, PEN-vs-range curve) should already
capture structurally via the K-factor/exponent fit, but is worth flagging as a real-world
confirmation that the in-game APDS PEN curve should not be treated as reliably superior at
long range without the corresponding accuracy penalty being modeled somewhere (this project
already separates PEN from to-hit, so this is likely already handled — flagged for
completeness, not as a new gap).

## Finding 3: the three historical validation vignettes (Wittmann, Tilly-sur-Seulles, Norrey-en-Bessin) do NOT appear in this book at all

A full page-by-page read (all 24 pages) found **no combat narrative content whatsoever** —
no named engagements, no unit-action anecdotes, no casualty or kill claims, nothing
resembling the Wittmann engagement, Tilly-sur-Seulles (14 June 1944), or Norrey-en-Bessin
(9 June 1944) vignettes already recorded in `sherman_firefly_1944.md`. This book is
structured as: (1) an 18-page technical/development history (origins, turret, hull,
production numbers, performance data — all covered in Findings above), then (2) a 16-page
photo-and-line-drawing reference section (pp.6-24) captioned entirely with equipment
descriptions ("Rear idler mounting," "Stowage in the driver's compartment," "The
ball-shaped muzzle brake," etc.) with only generic locational photo captions (e.g. "A New
Zealand Firefly in Italy," "Canadian troops passing new ammunition up to the turret of
their Firefly," "Every other tank is a Firefly — Canadian 5th Armoured Division in
Holland") — no dated, named combat incidents anywhere. **This means the three vignettes in
this project's existing research file are sourced from elsewhere, not from this Fletcher
title** — the "Fletcher via Wikipedia footnote" framing already used for the mantlet claim
likely applies here too, or those vignettes trace to a different, more narrative-focused
source (a candidate for future research: Fletcher's own longer Osprey New Vanguard 141
book, or a unit-history-focused title, neither of which is this one). This is a clean,
useful negative finding — it tells the project where NOT to expect vignette corroboration
from this specific title, narrowing the search for whoever's actual source those three
incidents came from.

## Finding 4: hull/turret construction cross-checks

- **Hull glacis, small-hatch, machine-gun deletion**: confirmed narratively and
  photographically. P.4 ("The Hull"): *"The most distinctive feature of the Firefly's hull,
  when viewed from the front, was the absence of the hull machine gun mounting and the
  blanking-off plate that covered the aperture. This signified the removal, not only of the
  gun, but the gunner, to make more room for ammunition. This reduced the crew from five on
  a normal Sherman to four on a Firefly."* Photos on pp.6, 10 (the blanked-off MG port, plus
  a working drawing of the armored cover plate with its own weld-spec callouts: 3/4" fillet
  weld, 1" fillet weld at the joint) independently confirm the physical modification. **No
  thickness figure given for the hull front plate itself anywhere in this book** — this is
  consistent with the book's general pattern of describing modifications/fittings rather
  than base-vehicle armor data, and does not contradict or refine this project's existing
  Hull Front figure (currently modeled per the M4A4-hull small-hatch convention, separate
  from the M4A1 cast-hull figure corrected earlier this session per design note E.133).
- **Turret bustle/radio box**: confirmed in detail, matching `sherman_firefly_1944.md`'s
  existing note. P.3-4: Witheridge's account of the origin of the rear turret extension box
  (counterbalance function primary, radio-stowage function an afterthought per his own
  account, contra Kilbourn/Brighty's version) — a real, sourced disagreement between two
  named participants about *why* the box was added, not just *that* it was added. No
  thickness figure given for the box's own armor (photos, p.18, show it as a plate/box
  structure, described only as "armored" with no mm figure) — this project's existing
  radio-bustle-box figure (51mm sides/62mm rear, cited to Sherman Minutia) is **not
  addressed or contradicted** by this book, since this book gives no numeric figure of its
  own to compare against.
- **Ammunition stowage total (77 rounds)**: this book gives a full, detailed breakdown (p.4)
  matching the "77 rounds officially" figure already in `sherman_firefly_1944.md`: 5 rounds
  in ready-use boxes beneath the gun, 20 rounds horizontally under the turret turntable
  (gunner's side), 20 rounds vertically in a floor-level bin at the turret front, 18 rounds
  at 40° under the turret floor (loader's side), and 14 rounds vertically in the redundant
  hull-MG-gunner's former position (rack capable of 15, stowage diagram specifies 14) — sums
  to 77 exactly (5+20+20+18+14=77). This is a clean, independent numeric confirmation of a
  figure this project already had, now with full positional detail available if ever needed
  for a more granular ammo-cookoff/vulnerability rule.
- **Turret ring / turret dimensions**: new data, not previously recorded — turret ring 56.0"
  inner diameter / 69.0" outer diameter; turret height 30.25" (one datum point) / 38.5" /
  31.25" (a second, unclear which reference line each uses without the full diagram key
  legible at this scan resolution). Recorded for completeness; not obviously actionable for
  any open project question, but available if a future need arises (e.g. modeling turret
  ring size as a factor in traverse speed or vulnerability).
- **Muzzle brake**: confirmed as a genuine recoil-mitigating muzzle brake (not a simple
  counterweight) — matches `sherman_firefly_1944.md`'s existing Sherman-Minutia-sourced
  finding. This book adds real independent detail: a labeled line drawing (p.16) titled
  "MUZZLE RECOIL BRAKE" showing two internal baffle chambers with "Locking nut" / "Locking
  ring" callouts, described in the ammunition-context text as producing "a vicious flash at
  the muzzle, exacerbated by the muzzle brake, which obscured the target" (p.5) — a real
  operational drawback (target obscuration on firing) not previously recorded in this
  project's Firefly research, worth flagging as flavor/design-note material even though it
  doesn't change any stat.
- **False muzzle brake / disguise measures**: new finding, not previously recorded. P.5
  describes several period field-expedient countermeasures against the Firefly's
  conspicuous long gun: painting the outer half of the barrel a lighter color; fitting a
  "false muzzle brake" partway down the barrel at the point where the paint color changed
  (to visually mimic a shorter 75mm gun's length); in Italy, fitting a dummy 75mm tube to
  the turret rear and driving with the real 17pdr reversed as much as possible; and using a
  conventional 75mm Sherman as a "stalking horse" to draw fire first. A photo (p.17) shows a
  "Firefly IC in Italy showing false muzzle brake and painted barrel" as a real fielded
  example, not just a proposal. This is genuinely new, useful flavor/scenario-design
  material (relevant to any future rule about target identification/threat prioritization by
  AI or scenario-design guidance) not previously captured in this project's Firefly file.

## Finding 5: production numbers and unit-holdings data (new, not previously recorded)

Page 4-5 gives a detailed Firefly production/fielding timeline: War Office initial
requirement 2,100 Shermans with the 17pdr (later raised to 3,100 by January 1945); main
conversion center was Royal Ordnance Factory, Barnbow, near Leeds (with Woolwich Arsenal
and Hayes cited in some other reports as additional sites); prototype turret proof-tested
at Woolwich Arsenal 26 December 1943; a report dated 24 June 1944 shows 288 Fireflies on
front-line strength in Normandy, with 699 converted by 31 July 1944; by 31 May 1945, 3,260
were on order, 2,139 issued. A detailed table (p.5) breaks down Firefly holdings by
division/brigade in 21st Army Group at end of June 1944 (7th Armoured 36, 11th Armoured 36,
Guards Armoured 36, Polish 1st Armoured 25, etc.) — useful context for scenario design
(force-composition guidance for mid-1944 OOBs) but not directly actionable for any current
armor-calc stat question. Recorded for completeness per this project's practice of
capturing genuinely new sourced data even when it doesn't resolve an open numeric question.

## Confidence Notes

- **Very high confidence** the "+13mm mantlet" claim does not appear in this book: based on
  a complete page-by-page read including every photo caption and diagram label, with the
  book's only mantlet-related content being functional description, two uncaptioned-for-
  dimensions photos, and two undimensioned line drawings.
- **High confidence** the three historical vignettes are absent from this book: same
  complete-read basis: this book contains zero named combat incidents of any kind.
- **High confidence** on the "summer of 1944" APDS timing figure and the 77-round stowage
  breakdown: both are direct, unambiguous statements/diagrams in the book's own running
  text, not inferred.
- **Moderate confidence** on the turret dimension figures (56.0"/69.0" turret ring,
  30.25"/38.5"/31.25" height figures): the numbers themselves are clearly printed, but which
  exact reference lines the smaller two height figures use could not be confirmed without a
  higher-resolution read of the diagram's own dimension-line key.
