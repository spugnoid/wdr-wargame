## Rifle Section

As of the April 1943 War Establishment (W.E. II/233/2), the British infantry **rifle section** was **10 men**, commanded by a Corporal, and organized into two functional groups built around a single Bren light machine gun:

- **Rifle Group (7 men):**
  - 1× Corporal (Section Commander) — armed with a **Sten** 9mm submachine gun (not a rifle; this was new as of the 1943 establishment — earlier in the war the section commander carried a rifle, then briefly a Thompson from late 1940).
  - 6× Rifleman (Private) — each armed with the **Lee-Enfield rifle** (Rifle No. 4 Mk I, or its predecessor the Short Magazine Lee-Enfield/SMLE Mk III, functionally equivalent, .303-inch calibre, 10-round magazine, bolt action).
- **Bren (Gun) Group (3 men):**
  - 1× Lance-Corporal (Section Second-in-Command) — Lee-Enfield rifle.
  - 1× Bren No. 1 (gunner) — the **Bren light machine gun** was his personal weapon (no separate rifle carried).
  - 1× Bren No. 2 (assistant/loader) — Lee-Enfield rifle, carried the spare barrel and bulk of the spare magazines.

**Totals per section:** 10 men; 1× Bren LMG; 1× Sten SMG (section commander only); 8× Lee-Enfield rifles.

This was the section as fought from spring 1943 onward. It had changed repeatedly earlier in the war — 8 men in 1937–1938 doctrine (even though a "10 men" establishment briefly existed on paper from April 1940, contemporary training literature said this was not meant to raise fighting strength above 8), 10 men (Corporal + 9 Privates) from June 1941, reduced to 9 under the 1942 Middle East establishment, then restored to 10 and given the current Rifle Group/Bren Group internal division and the Sten-armed commander under the April 1943 table.

## Other Squad-Level Units

The 10-man rifle section above is specific to the four **Rifle Companies** of the battalion. The battalion's **Support Company** (formalized under the same April 1943 reorganization) contained several other squad/detachment-sized building blocks with distinct compositions and weapons, worth noting as 1943-era variants even though they are not "rifle sections":

- **Assault Pioneer Section.** The Pioneer Platoon (renamed Assault Pioneer Platoon in 1943) consisted of a small Pioneer Section (tradesmen — bricklayer, mason, carpenter — with a 3-ton lorry) plus **two Assault Sections**, each of **4 pioneers (one a Corporal) plus a driver with a Jeep**. Equipped for mine clearance and obstacle destruction rather than rifle combat: 'Polish' mine detector sets, Bangalore torpedoes, No. 75 anti-tank grenades; the man-pack "Lifebuoy" flamethrower was associated with this platoon later in the war (from mid-1944) but issued from pooled stores, not held as standard equipment in 1943.
- **Carrier Section.** The Carrier Platoon's four Sections (April 1943 establishment) each fielded **3 Universal (Bren) Carriers with 13 men total** per section: carrier one crewed by a Serjeant, two Bren gunners ("LMG numbers"), and a driver-mechanic; carriers two and three each crewed by a Corporal, two Bren gunners, and a driver-mechanic; plus a motorcycle orderly armed with a Sten. Each carrier mounted a Bren gun; carrier one additionally carried a PIAT, carrier two a 2-inch mortar. This is a vehicle-borne fire-support team, not a dismounted rifle section, but is the closest Support Company analog in size to a section.
- **Anti-tank gun Detachment.** The Anti-tank Platoon (three Sections, each of two Detachments) fielded one towed **6-pounder anti-tank gun** and two Loyd carriers per Detachment; each Detachment additionally carried a Bren gun for local defense and a 2-inch mortar for smoke/illumination — again a specialist weapon-team, not a rifle section.
- **3-inch Mortar Detachment.** The Mortar Platoon (six mortars from 1941 onward) organized each mortar under a 5-man Detachment (commander plus three mortar numbers plus a carrier driver-mechanic from 1941).

None of these specialist teams replace or substitute for the standard rifle section within a Rifle Company; they are separate, Support-Company-held assets that existed alongside the standard rifle organization in 1943.

## Platoon Organization

Under the April 1943 War Establishment (W.E. II/233/2), the **rifle platoon** consisted of:

- **Three Rifle Sections**, 10 men each (30 men total), as described above.
- **Platoon Headquarters (7 men):**
  - 1× Subaltern (Platoon Commander, a Lieutenant or, more commonly, a 2nd Lieutenant) — armed with a revolver (Enfield or Webley, .38-inch, 6-round cylinder); from May 1944 an optional Sten machine carbine could additionally be issued to platoon commanders from a small pooled allocation, but this was not standard in 1943.
  - 1× Platoon Serjeant — Lee-Enfield rifle.
  - 1× Orderly (with bicycle) — Lee-Enfield rifle.
  - 1× Batman — Lee-Enfield rifle.
  - 1× Lance-Corporal — Lee-Enfield rifle.
  - 1× Mortar No. 1 — armed with a **Sten** SMG; operated the platoon's 2-inch mortar.
  - 1× Mortar No. 2 — Lee-Enfield rifle; assisted the 2-inch mortar.
  - Platoon HQ transport: one 15-cwt truck (for tools, ammunition, and crew-served-weapon stores; driver counted against the Administrative/Company transport pool, not the platoon's own manpower).

**Total platoon strength: 37 all ranks** (1 officer + 36 other ranks) — 1 pistol (revolver), 4 Sten submachine guns (platoon HQ's Mortar No.1 plus the three section commanders), 29 Lee-Enfield rifles, 3 Bren light machine guns (one per section), and **1× 2-inch (50.8mm) light mortar** organic to Platoon HQ.

There was no PIAT held permanently at platoon level in 1943 — PIATs were held centrally at Company Headquarters and issued down to platoons as the tactical situation required (see Company Organization, below).

## Company Organization

Under the same April 1943 establishment, the **Rifle Company** consisted of:

- **Three Rifle Platoons**, 37 all ranks each (111 total), as above.
- **Company Headquarters — 2 officers, 14 other ranks (16 total):**
  - Company Commander (usually a Major, occasionally a Captain).
  - Second-in-Command (Captain).
  - Company Serjeant-Major (CSM, Warrant Officer Class II).
  - Company Quartermaster-Serjeant (CQMS, Staff-Serjeant).
  - Two dedicated snipers — this was new as of the April 1943 reorganization (Rifle Company HQ had not carried dedicated snipers before). These two snipers per company were later (c. May 1944) consolidated with counterparts from the other three rifle companies into a single 8-man Sniper Section at Battalion Headquarters, so "2 snipers per rifle company" is specifically an April 1943–early 1944 feature, not one that held for the whole war.
  - Remaining Company HQ personnel: a clerk, a storeman, three orderlies, two batmen (each also a driver as of the 1943 table), and further dedicated drivers for company transport.
  - Company HQ transport: one Jeep (5-cwt), three 15-cwt trucks, and **one Universal (Bren) Carrier**, which itself mounted a Bren gun and a 2-inch mortar. (This carrier was an addition to Company HQ from 1943, provided for on the War Establishment rather than drawn from the battalion's Carrier Platoon.)

**Company-organic heavy weapon: three PIAT (Projector, Infantry, Anti-Tank) launchers**, held centrally at Company Headquarters and issued out to the three platoons as the tactical situation demanded — there was no standing PIAT crew; a section would detail one or two men to operate it when issued. The PIAT replaced the Boys anti-tank rifle (one per platoon HQ under the 1941 table, already dropped from the platoon in the 1942 Middle East reorganization) on an effectively one-for-one basis in mid-1943, but relocated the weapon's home from platoon to company level.

**Total company strength: 5 officers + approximately 122 other ranks ≈ 127 all ranks.** (This figure moved by one or two men across 1943–44 as driver/batman/sniper postings were juggled; contemporary sources give slightly different totals — e.g. 122–124 other ranks — depending on the exact date within that window.)

**What is explicitly NOT organic to the rifle company** (contrary to how "battalion support weapons" are sometimes loosely described): the **Vickers medium machine gun was not part of the standard infantry battalion at all** in the British Army of 1943 — not at company level, and not even at the battalion's Support Company level. Medium machine guns were concentrated in a separate **Divisional Machine Gun Battalion** (from 1942–44, sometimes reorganized as a "Divisional Support Battalion" that also carried 4.2-inch mortars and 20mm AA guns) held at division level, with Vickers platoons occasionally attached down to a rifle battalion for a specific operation rather than organic to it. The **3-inch mortar** (six per battalion) and the **6-pounder anti-tank gun** (six to eight per battalion) genuinely were organic — but at **battalion** level, in the Support Company's Mortar Platoon and Anti-tank Platoon respectively, not within the rifle company itself. The rifle company's own organic heavy-weapon allotment in 1943 was limited to the 2-inch mortars (one per platoon) and the three PIATs held at company HQ.

## Confidence Notes

- **Section composition (10 men, Rifle Group/Bren Group split, 1 Bren, 1 Sten, 8 rifles) — well-established.** Corroborated in near-identical detail by two independent secondary sources (bayonetstrength.uk, transcribing the actual W.E. II/233/2 table, and battleorder.org, an independently compiled OOB reference), plus general corroboration from a third (Colour Sergeant Tombstone's blog). High confidence for the April 1943 establishment specifically.
- **Platoon strength of 37 all ranks, with breakdown of weapons (1 pistol, 4 SMG, 29 rifle, 3 Bren, 1×2-inch mortar) — well-established, single primary-derived table but cross-checked against a second independent source.** The bayonetstrength.uk document reproduces the actual War Establishment personnel/weapon table (with a "No./Pistol/SMG/Rifle/Bren/2-inch" grid) for W.E. II/233/2 dated 30 April 1943, and battleorder.org's independently-written narrative account (37 personnel; 1 officer + 36 enlisted; 3 sections of 10; one 2-inch mortar in platoon HQ) matches it exactly. High confidence.
- **Company strength (~127 all ranks) and Company HQ composition — single-source-derived, moderate-high confidence.** The 5-officers/122-other-ranks figure and Company HQ role breakdown come primarily from battleorder.org's narrative summary, which itself appears derived from the same family of War Establishment documents used by bayonetstrength.uk; the two are consistent with each other but I did not independently verify the exact "16 all ranks in Company HQ" figure against a reproduced primary table (bayonetstrength.uk's own detailed table extraction for Company HQ was described narratively on p.27–28 of that PDF, not tabulated the way the Platoon page was). Treat the exact total (122 vs. 124 other ranks) as an approximation — sources themselves say the number moved by one or two men across 1943–44 depending on driver/batman/sniper posting changes.
- **Three PIATs held at Company HQ, issued to platoons as needed — well-established,** corroborated by both bayonetstrength.uk and battleorder.org in near-identical language, and consistent with general PIAT literature (its introduction in 1943 replacing the Boys anti-tank rifle).
- **Vickers MMG not organic to the infantry battalion (held instead in a Divisional MG Battalion) — well-established** via a third, independent source (Vickers MG Collection & Research Association) specifically consulted to check this point, which the initial task framing had assumed differently (as a battalion-level Support Company asset). This is treated as a correction, with moderately high confidence — the general principle is clear across sources, though the precise scale/timing of MG-platoon attachment down to a given rifle battalion in a given campaign season is not something this pass verified in depth.
- **Section commander's Sten and the Rifle-Group/Bren-Group internal split specifically as of April 1943 — well-established** but it is worth flagging that this was a relatively recent and still-evolving arrangement at that date (the Sten only replaced the Thompson as the section commander's personal weapon around this time; earlier and later establishments differ, e.g. Thompson from late 1940, brief general officer/NCO Sten trials in late 1942).
- **Colour Sergeant Tombstone blog's "26–30 all ranks" platoon estimate is treated as lower confidence** and is presented here mainly to flag that not every secondary source agrees on exact figures; the number derived from the actual War Establishment table (37) is preferred.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

- This document did not independently pull or inspect a scanned copy of the actual UK National Archives (Kew) War Office file for W.E. II/233/2 — the primary-source claims here rest on Gary Kennedy's (bayonetstrength.uk) transcription of that table, which is a careful and well-cited secondary compilation but is one step removed from the archival document itself. A follow-up pass could attempt to locate the WO 24 (or similar) series document at The National Archives (Kew) directly.
- The precise **company total** (all ranks) has some give in it across sources (122–124 other ranks cited); this pass did not resolve the discrepancy to a single authoritative figure for exactly 30 April 1943 versus slightly later 1943 amendments.
- **Regional/theatre variation** (Mediterranean vs. Northwest Europe vs. Home Forces) is only partially addressed. The April 1943 W.E. (II/233/2) is described as the establishment "used through the campaigns in Italy and Northwest Europe," but this pass did not check whether battalions already committed in North Africa/Sicily in mid-1943 had actually re-equipped to this establishment on the same timetable as UK-based battalions, or lagged behind on an older (e.g. Middle East VI/587) establishment for some months. Given the game's "representative 1943" framing this is likely a minor concern, but it is not fully closed.
- Whether/when the Bren No. 1 (gunner) was issued a personal sidearm (pistol) in addition to the Bren is inconsistent across the sources' historical narrative (a brief late-1942 policy of issuing Bren gunners a pistol is mentioned as "abandoned in 1943" by one source) — for April 1943 the answer appears to be "no personal sidearm, Bren counts as his only weapon," but this is a narrow, single-source detail.
- This pass did not address the **British Indian Army** infantry battalion, which used a different (and less well-documented, per Kennedy's own admission) organization; if the project later needs Indian Army/Gurkha organizational data for 1943, that is an explicit gap not covered here.
- Osprey's dedicated British-infantry-organization titles (e.g. *British Infantryman in North-West Europe 1944–45* or similar Men-at-Arms/Warrior series volumes) were referenced as a likely lead in the task brief but were not directly consulted in this pass (no accessible full text found via web search/fetch); the figures here rest instead on the bayonetstrength.uk/battleorder.org/Vickers MG Association sources. If the project has physical or library access to an Osprey title, it would be a good independent check against the figures above.
