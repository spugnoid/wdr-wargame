# Doyle & Jentz, *Sturmgeschütz III Assault Gun 1940-42* (Osprey New Vanguard 019) — Does It Cover the Ausf G?

Full read (50 pages, image scan, no OCR text layer) of Hilary Doyle & Tom Jentz (text),
Peter Sarson (color plates), *Sturmgeschütz III Assault Gun 1940-42* (Osprey Fighting
Armor of WWII, Military Book Club edition, 1996, ISBN 1-85532-537-3), specifically to
check whether it independently corroborates the StuG III Ausf G layered-front-armor
correction already applied this session (design note E.138, `stug3_panzer_tracts_1943.md`),
or supplies any Top/Roof armor data for the Ausf G, or contains the Hauptmann Peter
Frantz/Stanowoje (14 March 1943) anecdote flagged by `panzer3_top_armor_1943.md`.

## Short answer: this book's own scope stops at the Ausf E (early 1942) — it never reaches the Ausf G at all

The title is literal, not a publisher's rounding: the book's own text states the
**Ausf.E** was "replaced by the Ausf.F in March 1942" (p.47, Plate F caption), and the
book's last production model, last color plate (Plate F/G, Ausf.E, Sturmgeschütz-
Abteilung 249 and 197, both 1942), and last specifications table all stop there. **No
Ausf.F, Ausf.F/8, or Ausf.G is mentioned, illustrated, or described anywhere in this
book.** This project's roster models the Ausf G specifically, so this source cannot
speak to it directly — a scope mismatch, not a search failure, exactly as suspected in
`panzer3_top_armor_1943.md`'s Open Question #3 before this book was read.

## Findings

1. **Ausf G coverage — absent.** Confirmed by a full page-by-page read. The book's
   own internal chronology (production history, "Table 1: Sturmgeschütz production
   history," combat narrative through "Early Success in Russia" covering 1941-42, and
   the color-plate roster) ends at the Ausf.E, delivered September 1941-March 1942.

2. **Top/Roof armor — one figure found, but explicitly for the early Ausf.A-E series,
   not the Ausf G.** Page 6 (read in the earlier pass) gives a full armor rundown for
   the original pre-appliqué series: *"50 mm thick driver's front plate at an angle of
   9° from vertical, 50 mm hull front at 20° and 50°, 30 mm superstructure and hull
   sides at 0°, 30 mm tail plates at 10° and 30°, 10 mm fighting compartment roof at
   77–90°, 16 mm rear deck at 80–87°, and 15 mm belly plate at 90°."* No later
   passage revises or restates this figure for any subsequent production block covered
   by the book (Ausf.B through E), and none of the book's diagrams (the two labeled
   cutaways, Plates D and Ausf.B fold-out) include a roof-thickness callout in their
   printed keys. **Not usable for the Ausf G**, whose roof geometry the project's own
   `vehicle_top_armor_1943.md` already flags as structurally different (a sloped
   middle roof section unique to the G). Recorded as a data point for the *early*
   series only, consistent with (not independently new versus) what was already
   captured from this book's earlier pages.

3. **Layered front-armor construction (base + bolt-on face-hardened applique) —
   independently corroborated in general terms, but for the wrong production point to
   refine the Ausf G's own figure.** Page 38 states plainly: *"The practice of bolting
   on additional armour with cylindrical bolts did not present a significant hazard,
   was quite common on several models of the Pz.Kpfw.III, Pz.Kpfw.IV and Sturmgeschütz,
   and continued to the end of the war with every Sturmgeschütz Ausf.G."* This is a
   real, independent, named confirmation that the Ausf G specifically used bolted-on
   applique armor (matching the general mechanism already modeled via
   `layered_plate_effective_thickness()` per design note E.138) — but it is a single
   narrative sentence with no thickness figures of its own, so it **cannot refine**
   the existing 50mm base + 30mm applique = 64.2mm effective figure already in
   `vehicles.csv`. Treated as corroborating narrative support, not a numeric source.

4. **Hauptmann Peter Frantz / Stanowoje anecdote — not found.** No mention of this
   officer, action, or date appears anywhere in this book's combat narrative sections
   (which cover May-June 1940 France and June-December 1941 Russia in detail, via named
   unit experience reports from Sturmgeschütz-Abteilung 185, 192, and others). Given
   this book's own chronology ends before March 1943, this is expected, not a
   contradiction — the anecdote's date postdates this book's entire narrative scope.

5. **Hull deck/floor data** — the same page-6 figure above (16mm rear deck, 15mm belly
   plate) is the only floor/deck data in the book, and it carries the same early-series
   caveat as Finding 2. No distinct figure for later production blocks or the Ausf G.

## Confidence Notes

- **High confidence** this book's scope never reaches the Ausf G: confirmed by a
  complete page-by-page read, the book's own explicit statement that Ausf.E was
  replaced by Ausf.F in March 1942, and the total absence of any Ausf.F/G reference,
  illustration, or specification anywhere in the text or plates.
- **High confidence** the p.6 armor figure (including the 10mm/77-90° roof figure) is
  scoped to the early Ausf.A-E series specifically, not a general StuG III constant —
  it appears embedded in a paragraph describing that specific production run's
  characteristics, with no restatement or revision for later variants within this
  book's own (limited) chronology.
- **Moderate-high confidence** in the p.38 "every Sturmgeschütz Ausf.G" applique-armor
  sentence as genuine corroboration of the general bolt-on mechanism — but it is a
  single retrospective sentence in a book otherwise about 1940-42 vehicles, not a
  first-hand technical description of the Ausf G itself.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

1. StuG III Ausf G's own Top/Roof armor figure remains unresolved. This book, like
   every other book read this session, does not supply it. The early-series 10mm/
   77-90° figure (this book, p.6) remains a plausible but unconfirmed proxy — the same
   status it already held before this pass, now simply ruled out as improvable by this
   particular source.
2. The Hauptmann Peter Frantz/Stanowoje (14 March 1943) anecdote remains real,
   specific, and dated, but still uncorroborated by any second source and still
   missing the gun-type/range/target detail a Rule 18.12 entry would need. This book
   does not help (its narrative ends before the event's date).
