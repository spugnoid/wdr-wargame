# Perrett, *Sturmartillerie & Panzerjäger 1939-45* — Does It Answer the PaK 40 Questions?

Research pass reading Bryan Perrett, *Sturmartillerie & Panzerjäger 1939-45* (Osprey
New Vanguard 34, 1999), in full (48 pages of body text plus index/back matter),
specifically to check whether it resolves this project's two still-open PaK 40
questions: crew quality tier and the standard division's 1943 PaK 38→PaK 40
conversion date (both left open by `counters/toe/pak40_1943.md` and its follow-up,
`counters/toe/pak40_crew_quality_and_fielding_date_followup.md`, design notes E.123
and E.130).

## Short answer: no, on both counts — this book is about a different branch entirely

**This is the exact branch-conflation trap the follow-up research file explicitly
warned about, confirmed from the primary source's own table of contents.** The
book's "Panzerjäger" is the **self-propelled tank destroyer vehicle family** —
Panzerjäger I, Marder I/II/III, Nashorn, Elefant/Ferdinand, Jagdpanzer IV, Hetzer,
Jagdpanther, Jagdtiger — never the towed 7.5cm PaK 40 as a standalone weapon system
with its own crew and organic infantry-division battalion. The towed PaK 40 appears
in this book exactly once, as a **gun mounted on a self-propelled carriage**: "Later
models of the Marder III mounted the German 75mm L46 Pak 40/3 gun" (p.23). It is
never discussed as a towed weapon in a gun pit with its own crew.

Both organizational chapters in the book ("MANNING AND ORGANISATION," p.11, and
"TANK DESTROYERS"/"TANK DESTROYER TACTICS," pp.21, 34) and the four order-of-battle
charts (pp.42-43: Assault Gun Battery 1940, Assault Gun Battalion 1941/1942/1944-45,
Assault Artillery Brigade 1944-45, 653/654 Heavy Tank Destroyer Battalion at Kursk
1943, generic Heavy Tank Destroyer Battalion) are entirely about **assault gun
battalions** (StuG III/IV, organic to Panzergrenadier/infantry divisions as fire
support) and **heavy tank destroyer battalions** (Elefant, Nashorn, Jagdpanther —
30-38 vehicles, army-troop assets committed as needed). Neither structure is the
standard infantry division's own towed Panzerjäger-Abteilung this project is trying
to date — that unit type (organic to a standard infantry division, towed 5cm PaK 38
transitioning to 7.5cm PaK 40) is not covered by this book at all.

## Question 1 (crew quality): not answered for PaK 40; answered for the wrong branch (Sturmgeschütz)

No statement anywhere in this book rates towed PaK 40/Panzerjäger-Abteilung crew
quality. What the book **does** state, clearly, is for **Sturmartillerie (assault
gun) crews specifically**, a different branch entirely (p.11): **"Assault
artillerymen were all volunteers and were considered to be the élite of their
service. As well as gaining numerous awards of the Iron Cross First and Second
Class, the Assault Artillery branch won no less than 325 German Crosses in Gold and
140 Knight's Crosses, 14 holders of the latter being further awarded Oakleaves."**
This is real, specific, and well-sourced for the *assault gun* branch (StuG III/IV,
already a separate roster entry in this project) — but it says nothing about towed
anti-tank gun crews, and using it for the PaK 40 would be exactly the kind of
cross-branch misapplication the follow-up research file already flagged as a trap
to avoid.

**`GER_PAK40_1943.3_F`'s "regular" quality tier remains exactly as unsourced as
before this pass.**

## Question 2 (fielding date): not answered — wrong unit type covered

The book gives detailed lineage for assault gun battalion/brigade organization
(1940 through 1944-45) and for the heavy tank destroyer battalions that emerged in
1943 (Elefant at Kursk, then Nashorn/Jagdpanther), but never mentions the standard
infantry division's own towed antitank battalion or its 1943 PaK 38→PaK 40
conversion at all. One tangential, non-committal data point: p.34 notes "Guderian's
decision that the assault gun and the tank destroyer would gradually merge their
roles was implemented throughout the introduction of this second generation of tank
destroyers [1944], a large proportion of the crews being drawn from the Assault
Artillery" — an administrative consolidation of the *self-propelled* branches, not
the towed-gun infantry battalions, and not dated more precisely than "1944."

**The October 1943 candidate window from the Nafziger Collection (design note
E.130) remains the best lead found so far, still unconfirmed.**

## Crew size: not given for PaK 40 (not covered); given for several self-propelled vehicles instead

No crew figure for the towed PaK 40 gun team. Crew sizes given for the
self-propelled vehicles the book *does* cover, for reference (all four-to-five man
vehicle crews, not directly comparable to this project's 6-man towed-gun figure
since these represent a different weapon system entirely — a fighting compartment
crew, not a full gun-serving detachment): Marder II — crew of 4 (p.23); Marder III
— crew of 4 (p.23); Nashorn — crew of 5 (p.24); Jagdpanzer IV — crew of 4 (p.33);
Hetzer — crew of 4 (p.34); Jagdpanther — crew of 5 (p.34).

## Bonus finding: a real, dated, well-documented Sturmgeschütz (StuG III) anecdote — not PaK 40, but usable if this project ever wants one for StuG III

Pages 19-21 give an extended, well-sourced account of Sturmgeschütz Battery/
Abteilung 667's actions, all independently dated and citation-worthy if this
project ever wants a Rule 18.12-style historical validation entry for its existing
StuG III roster row:

- **12-19 September 1941, Leningrad area**: Battery 667 (then a battery, later
  expanded to a full Abteilung) supported the German I Corps' breakthrough; Oblt.
  Joachim Lutzow awarded the Knight's Cross, cited for "6,500 prisoners and 92
  guns... 225 modern bunkers and 301 heavy weapon and machine-gun posts destroyed."
- **9 September 1942, Rzhew sector**: 3rd Battery, reduced to 5 effective vehicles
  and nearly surrounded, destroyed 33 attacking T-34s in a single action; battery
  commander Oblt. Baurmann awarded the German Cross in Gold.
- **28-31 August 1942, same sector**: the battalion destroyed 83 enemy tanks in
  four days.
- **By end of 1943, per the book's own estimate**: assault guns alone had destroyed
  13,000 enemy tanks; by spring 1944, 20,000 (p.20) — a general branch-wide claim,
  not specific to one vehicle or engagement, offered without a named primary
  citation in the book itself (Perrett doesn't footnote this figure).

These are strong, specific, dated anecdotes for **Sturmgeschütz/assault gun crews**
specifically — not usable for a PaK 40 validation entry, but flagged here since this
project already has a StuG III roster row and might want this material later.

## Confidence Notes

- **The branch-mismatch finding itself: high confidence.** The entire book was read
  (48 pages), not sampled — this isn't "the PaK 40 material wasn't found in a
  skim," it's "this book's own structure and scope never includes the towed PaK 40
  as a subject in its own right."
- **The Sturmartillerie élite/volunteer claim (p.11): high confidence as a direct
  quotation**, but it's for a different branch and this pass takes no position on
  whether or how it might inform a future StuG III-specific quality-tier decision.
- **Self-propelled vehicle crew sizes: high confidence as direct quotations**, but
  explicitly not comparable to the towed PaK 40's own crew-size question.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

1. **The PaK 40 crew-quality and fielding-date questions remain exactly as open as
   design note E.130 left them.** This book does not move either question forward.
   The strongest unexploited leads remain the ones already named there: Forczyk's
   *Panzerjäger vs KV-1* (its "organization and training of the combatants"
   section, still unread as of this pass) and the Nafziger Collection's actual
   document content (still only the index has been seen).
2. **If this project ever wants a proper crew-quality source for its existing StuG
   III roster row**, p.11's "assault artillerymen were all volunteers... élite of
   their service" claim is real, specific, and directly quotable — a genuinely
   different and better-sourced situation than the PaK 40's own still-empty
   crew-quality question, just for a different vehicle.
3. **The 653/654 Heavy Tank Destroyer Battalion order of battle at Kursk (p.43)**
   is a real, sourced organizational chart for the Elefant — not directly useful
   for this project's current roster (no Elefant/Ferdinand modeled), but flagged
   in case that ever changes.
