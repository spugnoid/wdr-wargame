# The Encyclopedia of Weapons of World War II (general reference)

## Sources

- *The Encyclopedia of Weapons of World War II* (Salamander-style single-volume
  general reference), 544pp. Real OCR text layer (ABBYY FineReader), searched
  via `pdftotext` — not an image scan.
- Organized as short narrative articles per vehicle/gun/ship, most ending in a
  "Specification" block (Crew/Weight/Powerplant/Dimensions/Performance).
- This pass checked the book against eight specific open questions pulled from
  this project's own research files (see below), not a general survey.

## Findings — a clean negative across every item checked

**Governing structural fact**: every AFV "Specification" block in this book
gives only Crew/Weight/Powerplant/Dimensions/Performance — it never lists
armor thickness by plate or location for any tank, anywhere (spot-checked
Panzer III, Panzer IV, Cromwell, Churchill, Crusader entries). Armor appears
only as a single overall range in prose (e.g. Churchill "16-102mm in Mks
I-VI," Cromwell "8-76mm," T-34 "minimum of 18mm and maximum of 60mm"). This
book is not a source for plate-level armor geometry of any kind.

1. **T-34/85 top armor** — clean negative. No dedicated T-34/85 armor
   breakdown beyond the general "18-60mm" range for the whole T-34 line.
2. **SU-85 top armor** — clean negative, and stronger than expected: there is
   no dedicated SU-85 entry at all, only a one-sentence mention under the
   85mm AA gun article.
3. **T-34 Model 1943 hexagonal-turret roof** — clean negative. The book
   confirms the hexagonal-turret/wider-mantlet model's existence but gives no
   roof thickness.
4. **Panzer III top armor** — clean negative. Full narrative and spec block,
   no armor breakdown.
5. **Cromwell hull top/turret roof, nose-plate conflict** — clean negative,
   only the overall "8-76mm" range given.
6. **Churchill stepped-glacis middle/lower plate angles** — clean negative,
   only overall "16-102mm"/"25-152mm" ranges by mark.
7. **US 57mm M1 / 6pdr K-factor or muzzle velocity** — no dedicated 6pdr or
   US 57mm M1 towed-gun article found despite an extensive search (German
   Pak 38/Pak 40 do have full ballistic spec blocks with muzzle velocity and
   penetration tables; no Allied towed AT gun equivalent turned up). Treated
   as likely absent rather than exhaustively proven absent — a targeted
   re-check of this specific book is not worth another pass, but this is a
   softer negative than the others.
8. **PaK 40 crew quality / 1943 divisional fielding date** — clean negative,
   as expected going in; the Pak 40 article is purely technical/production
   history, no organizational or crew data.

## Confidence Notes

- This is a real text-layer book, correctly and thoroughly searched — the
  negative results are genuine absences in the source, not an extraction
  failure.
- The book's actual strength (narrative/production-history flavor text,
  general specification data) does not overlap with this project's current
  category of open questions, which are almost entirely plate-level armor
  geometry and crew-quality assessments. It is not a useful target for
  further armor-geometry research passes.
