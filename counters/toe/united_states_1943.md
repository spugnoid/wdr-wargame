# United States — 1943 Infantry Table of Organization and Equipment

## Sources

- War Department, **Table of Organization and Equipment (T/O&E) 7-17, "Infantry Rifle Company"**, originally effective **1 April 1942**. This is the T/O&E structure that governed the standard (non-armored, non-airborne) U.S. Army rifle company for essentially all of 1943; it was superseded by a revised T/O&E 7-17 dated **26 February 1944**, which itself was amended 30 June 1944. Digitized scan of the 26 Feb 1944 edition consulted directly: militaryresearch.org, [T/O&E 7-17, 26 February 1944](https://www.militaryresearch.org/7-17%2026Feb44.pdf) (personnel-roster table reviewed via OCR/text extraction; the equipment annex did not extract cleanly from this scan — see Open Questions).
- War Department Field Manual **FM 7-10, "Rifle Company, Infantry Regiment"** (edition of 18 March 1944, as referenced by secondary sources below). This edition postdates 1943 by a few months but describes the same organizational lineage; an earlier 1942 edition of FM 7-10 exists but was not independently located/consulted for this document (see Open Questions).
- Battleorder.org, **"U.S. Army Rifle Company Organization (1942-1943)"**, https://www.battleorder.org/usa-riflecoy-1942 — a secondary compilation citing War Department T/O&E 7-17 (1 April 1942) directly, with a full personnel-by-position breakdown for squad/platoon/company/weapons platoon. Used as the primary basis for the 1943 figures in this document because it gives the clearest itemized breakdown by position and weapon.
- Battleorder.org, **"U.S. Army Rifle Company (1944-45)"**, https://www.battleorder.org/post/usa-rifleco-1944 — companion page covering the 26 Feb 1944 T/O&E 7-17 revision and the 30 June 1944 amendment. Used here only for contrast, to identify which specific details changed from 1943 to 1944 (so that 1943 figures are not accidentally contaminated with 1944 changes). Cites FM 7-10 (18 Mar 1944) and John B. Sayen, "Battalion: An Organizational Study of the United States Infantry" (Marine Corps Combat Development Command working paper, 2001).
- Trailblazersww2.org, **"History: Infantry Structure"**, https://www.trailblazersww2.org/history_infantrystructure.htm, attributed to LTC Hugh Foster (USA, Ret.), dated 26 April 2000 — secondary source, used for cross-checking. Its squad-level description agrees with the above; its platoon/company strength totals differ somewhat from Battleorder's (see Confidence Notes / Open Questions).
- Wikipedia, "M7 grenade launcher," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M7_grenade_launcher — used only to confirm the timing of the M7 grenade-launcher adapter's adoption for the M1 Garand (1943) versus the earlier M1 launcher used on the M1903 Springfield, which is relevant to why the 1943 squad's grenadier still carried a bolt-action rifle.
- Cross-check performed against Shelby L. Stanton, *Order of Battle: U.S. Army, World War II* (Stackpole Books), which is widely cited as an authoritative secondary reference for U.S. Army T/O&E figures of this period; the book itself was not directly text-searched (no full-text version located), but summaries of its squad-organization content found via web search are consistent with the figures below.
- War Department T/O&E 7-25 (Armored Infantry Battalion, 15 September 1943) and T/O&E 7-27 (Rifle Company, Armored Infantry Battalion, 15 September 1943) were located and briefly reviewed for context but describe the **armored** infantry rifle company, a distinct organization from the standard infantry rifle company that is the subject of this document. Not used for figures below.

## Rifle Squad

The 1943 U.S. Army **rifle squad** (T/O&E 7-17, 1 Apr 1942 organization, in force through 1943) had an authorized strength of **12 men**:

| Position | Count | Personal weapon |
|---|---|---|
| Squad Leader (Sergeant) | 1 | M1 Garand rifle |
| Assistant Squad Leader (Corporal) | 1 | M1 Garand rifle |
| Automatic Rifleman (BAR gunner) | 1 | M1918A2 Browning Automatic Rifle (BAR) |
| Assistant Automatic Rifleman | 1 | M1 Garand rifle |
| Ammunition Bearer (for BAR) | 1 | M1 Garand rifle |
| Rifle Grenadier | 1 | M1903/M1903A3 Springfield rifle + M1 rifle grenade launcher |
| Riflemen (two of whom served as scouts) | 6 | M1 Garand rifle |

**Weapon totals per squad:** 10 M1 Garand rifles, 1 M1903-series Springfield (grenadier only), 1 M1918A2 BAR. No submachine gun was part of the standard rifle squad's organic issue in 1943.

The squad leader's personal weapon was the **M1 Garand rifle**, not the M1 Carbine — the carbine in this organization was reserved for platoon/company officers and certain specialist NCOs (drivers, buglers, communications personnel), not the rifle squad leader.

Note on the grenadier's weapon: in 1943 the rifle grenade launcher (M1 launcher) was mounted on the bolt-action M1903 Springfield rather than the semi-automatic M1 Garand, because the Garand-compatible **M7 grenade launcher** adapter was only adopted in 1943 and had not yet been reflected in the T/O&E's small-arms allocation; the squad still carried one M1903 for this purpose. This changed in the 26 February 1944 T/O&E revision, which reassigned rifle-grenade duty to two grenadiers, both now armed with M1 Garand rifles fitted with the M7 launcher (see Confidence Notes).

## Other Squad-Level Units

There was no separate "weapons squad" organic to the rifle platoon in 1943 — light machine guns and 60mm mortars were held one echelon up, at **company** level in the Weapons Platoon (see below), not at platoon level. The rifle platoon itself consisted only of a platoon headquarters and three (identical) rifle squads as described above.

At company level, the Weapons Platoon was organized into two distinct squad types, both feeding down from the company's weapons platoon rather than being organic to a rifle platoon:

- **Light Machine Gun Squad** (2 per company, grouped into one Light Machine Gun Section): each squad had a Squad Leader (Corporal), a Gunner (M1919A4 .30-cal light/air-cooled machine gun + M1911A1 pistol), an Assistant Gunner (M1911A1 pistol), and 2 Ammunition Bearers — roughly 5 men per squad, one M1919A4 gun each.
- **60mm Mortar Squad** (3 per company, grouped into one 60mm Mortar Section): each squad had a Squad Leader (Corporal), a Gunner (60mm M2 mortar + M1911A1 pistol), an Assistant Gunner (M1911A1 pistol), and 2 Ammunition Bearers — roughly 5 men per squad, one 60mm mortar each.

These are company-echelon weapons squads, not a platoon-level "weapons squad" — the rifle platoon in 1943 had no organic crew-served weapon of its own beyond the BAR embedded in each rifle squad.

## Platoon Organization

The 1943 **rifle platoon** consisted of a Platoon Headquarters and three Rifle Squads, for a total authorized strength of **1 officer + 45 enlisted = 46 all ranks**:

**Platoon Headquarters (1 officer, 9 enlisted):**
- Platoon Commander (Lieutenant) — M1 Carbine
- Platoon Sergeant (Staff Sergeant) — M1 Garand rifle
- Platoon Guide (Sergeant) — M1 Garand rifle
- 2 Messengers — M1 Garand rifles
- 5 "Basic" duty privates — M1 Garand rifles

**Combat element:** 3 Rifle Squads × 12 men = 36.

The rifle platoon had **no organic 60mm mortar and no organic bazooka/rocket launcher** in 1943 — both of those weapon types sat one echelon up, in the company's Weapons Platoon (mortars) or were entirely absent from the rifle company's organic equipment (bazookas; see Company Organization below).

(Note: the platoon headquarters was slimmed down from 10 to 5 positions — dropping the 5 "basic" privates — in the 26 February 1944 revision, which correspondingly reduced platoon strength to 41. The 46-man figure above is specific to the 1943 organization and should not be confused with the smaller 1944 platoon.)

## Company Organization

The 1943 **rifle company** consisted of a Company Headquarters, three Rifle Platoons, and one Weapons Platoon, for a total authorized strength of **6 officers + 192 enlisted = 198**, plus 3 attached medical aid men from the regimental medical detachment (201 with attachments).

**Company Headquarters (2 officers, 19 enlisted, +3 medical attachments):** Captain (commander), 1st Lieutenant (executive officer), First Sergeant, Supply Sergeant, Communications Sergeant, Company Clerk, Armorer, Bugler, 4 Messengers, Orderly, plus a Mess Section (Mess Sergeant, 4 cooks, 3 cook's helpers). Personal weapons were mostly M1 Carbine (command/specialist staff) or M1 Garand (support personnel).

**3 Rifle Platoons** as described above (46 men each, 138 total).

**Weapons Platoon (1 officer, ~38 enlisted):**
- Platoon Headquarters: Platoon Commander (Lieutenant, M1 Carbine), Platoon Sergeant, Transport Corporal, 2 Messengers, 2 Drivers; 2 jeeps with trailers for transport.
- **Light Machine Gun Section**: section headquarters (3 men) + 2 Light Machine Gun Squads (~5 men each) = **2 organic M1919A4 .30-cal light machine guns**.
- **60mm Mortar Section**: section headquarters (4 men) + 3 Mortar Squads (~5 men each) = **3 organic 60mm mortars**.

**Organic company-level heavy weapons in 1943, precisely stated:** 2× M1919A4 light machine guns and 3× 60mm mortars, both held in the company's own Weapons Platoon — these are organic to the rifle company itself, not attached from the battalion. The .30-cal **heavy** (tripod, water-cooled M1917A1) machine guns and 81mm mortars belonged to the separate **Heavy Weapons Company** (T/O&E 7-18) at **battalion** level and were only attached down to a rifle company as needed for a specific operation; they were not part of the rifle company's own table of organization.

**Bazookas:** The 1943 rifle company, per the sources reviewed, had **no organic bazooka (2.36-inch rocket launcher) allocation at all** — no bazooka appears in the Battleorder.org itemized 1942-1943 breakdown of company HQ, weapons platoon HQ, or any squad. A pool of 5 bazookas (plus 6 spare BARs and 6 Thompson SMGs) held at Company Headquarters for discretionary issue is well documented, but only as an amendment dated **30 June 1944** — i.e., after the period this document covers. Anti-tank rifle grenades (fired from the M1903 grenadier's launcher) were the rifle company's only organic anti-armor capability in 1943.

## Confidence Notes

- **Rifle squad size (12 men) and BAR count (1 per squad):** Well established. Multiple independent sources (Battleorder.org citing T/O&E 7-17 directly, Trailblazersww2.org, and general secondary summaries of Shelby Stanton's *Order of Battle*) agree without exception.
- **Squad position breakdown (leader/asst. leader/BAR team/grenadier/riflemen) and personal weapons:** Well established from the Battleorder.org itemized breakdown, which cites the T/O&E directly. High confidence.
- **Grenadier armed with M1903 Springfield (not M1 Garand) in 1943, changing to two M1-Garand-armed grenadiers in Feb 1944:** Single clearly-sourced claim (Battleorder.org's explicit before/after comparison), corroborated indirectly by the independently-confirmed 1943 introduction date of the M7 grenade launcher (Wikipedia/general small-arms references). Treated as reliable but effectively single-source for the specific "1943 vs 1944" framing.
- **Platoon strength of 46 (1943) vs. 41 (1944):** The 1943 figure (46) is derived by summing Battleorder.org's own itemized position list, which is internally consistent (10-man platoon HQ + 36-man squads = 46, matching its own stated "1 officer, 45 enlisted"). However, Trailblazersww2.org states an "Authorized Strength: 41" for what it labels as the "1943-44" platoon — this actually matches the smaller, **1944** platoon HQ (5 positions, not 10). This is very likely a case of a secondary source blurring the 1943/1944 boundary rather than a genuine disagreement about the 1943 figure itself, but it is flagged here as a single-source figure (46) rather than one confirmed by two independent tallies.
- **Company strength of 198 (1943) vs. 193 (1944):** Similarly, the 192-enlisted 1943 figure comes from a single itemized secondary source (Battleorder.org 1942-43 page); the 1944-45 page's total of 187 enlisted is lower, consistent with the platoon-HQ downsizing described above. This is treated as well-supported but single-source for the exact enlisted count.
- **2 organic light machine guns and 3 organic 60mm mortars at company level:** Well established — both the 1943 and 1944 Battleorder.org breakdowns agree exactly (2 LMG squads × 1 gun, 3 mortar squads × 1 tube), and this matches the general secondary-literature consensus that the WWII U.S. rifle company weapons platoon had exactly two machine-gun squads and three mortar squads. An earlier, less-detailed web search result suggested "six light machine guns" at company level; this was checked against two independently-detailed organizational breakdowns and rejected as almost certainly a confusion with a different unit or echelon (possibly the battalion Heavy Weapons Company, which held more machine guns). Treated as resolved with high confidence in favor of 2 LMGs.
- **No organic bazooka in 1943; 5-bazooka company HQ pool added 30 June 1944:** The "no bazooka in 1943" conclusion rests on the absence of any bazooka line in a detailed, itemized 1942-1943 breakdown, plus an explicit statement elsewhere that the 5-bazooka pool was "added 30 June 1944." This is treated as reasonably solid (an itemized source that lists everything else in detail and omits bazookas is meaningful evidence of absence), but it is technically an argument from omission in one source rather than a source that positively states "the rifle company had zero bazookas in 1943," so it is flagged as slightly less certain than the weapon-count figures above.
- **Squad leader rank (Sergeant in 1943, Staff Sergeant in 1944) and assistant squad leader rank (Corporal in 1943, Sergeant in 1944):** Consistent between the two Battleorder.org pages, treated as reliable, though rank is not load-bearing for this document's stated purpose and was not cross-checked further.
- **Primary-document verification:** A scanned copy of T/O&E 7-17 (26 Feb 1944, i.e., the successor document) was directly retrieved and its personnel-roster table reviewed via text extraction, which corroborates the general shape of the organization (company HQ + 3 rifle platoons + weapons platoon with a 60mm mortar section of 3 squads and a light machine gun section of 2 squads). However, the OCR/text extraction was not clean enough to independently re-derive exact headcounts or the equipment annex, so the figures in this document rely primarily on the secondary compilations rather than a from-scratch primary-document reading. A genuinely 1943-dated (rather than 1 Apr 1942 or 26 Feb 1944) scan of T/O&E 7-17 itself was not located.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

- No scan of a T/O&E 7-17 edition actually dated within 1943 was located — the two primary-document dates found bracketing 1943 are 1 April 1942 and 26 February 1944. It is well-attested that only "minor changes" occurred in this window and that the 1 Apr 1942 organization (as described by Battleorder.org's "1942-1943" page) is the one that applied through 1943, but a reader wanting an actual 1943-dated primary document should keep looking for a possible interim revision (e.g., a 1943 change bulletin to T/O&E 7-17) that was not found in this pass.
- The exact rank of the rifle squad leader (Sergeant vs. Staff Sergeant) varies between sources depending on which year's organization they describe; this document uses "Sergeant" for 1943 based on Battleorder.org's 1942-1943 page specifically, but this could not be triple-checked against a primary 1943 document.
- The Weapons Platoon Headquarters enlisted count in the source used (Battleorder.org 1942-1943) has an internal arithmetic wrinkle: the stated header total ("1 Officer, 38 Enlisted") does not cleanly sum from the itemized position list as presented (platoon HQ enlisted + LMG section + mortar section sums closer to ~31 by this document's recount). This may reflect additional positions not fully itemized on that page (e.g., additional ammunition/basic privates) rather than an error in the 38 figure itself, but it was not possible to resolve definitively without the primary equipment/personnel annex. Treat the ~38 total as approximate; the squad-level weapon counts (2 LMG, 3 mortars) are the figures with the strongest support.
- Whether the "2 Drivers" in the Weapons Platoon Headquarters were actually issued M1918A2 BARs personally (as one secondary source states) rather than carbines/rifles is unusual and was not independently corroborated; flagged as a low-confidence detail not otherwise used in this document's summary tables.
- An early-war "Automatic Rifle Squad" as a distinct 4th squad type at platoon level (separate from 3 uniform rifle squads) is referenced obliquely in one source describing pre-1943 changes ("getting rid of the Automatic Rifle Squad simplified things..."), suggesting an even earlier (1940-41) organizational stage existed with a dedicated automatic-weapons squad. This predates the 1943 period in scope here and was not researched further; flagged only in case it becomes relevant to earlier-war scenarios.
- A true 1942 edition of FM 7-10 was not located/consulted; only the 18 March 1944 edition was available via secondary citation. The 1943 organizational facts in this document rest on the T/O&E lineage and its secondary compilations rather than on FM 7-10 doctrine text itself, so FM 7-10 should be treated as background context rather than a verified source for the specific figures above.
