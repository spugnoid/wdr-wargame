# Deutsche Panzer 1917-1945 — Uwe Feist (Action Publications, 1978)

## Sources

- Uwe Feist, *Deutsche Panzer 1917-1945* (Action Publications, 1978),
  102pp. Bilingual (German/English) photo-and-spec-sheet compendium. Image
  scan, no OCR text layer (`pdftotext` returned nothing) — read via direct
  page-image inspection, ~25 pages sampled covering the full Panzer III
  production run (A/D1/E/F/G/H/J/L/M/N), the full StuG III run (A through
  the late Ausf.G "Abschlussausführung"/10.5cm Sturmhaubitze variant),
  Panzer IV A through H, plus color camouflage-profile plates near the end
  (Jagdpanther, Hetzer, Elefant).
- This pass checked four specific open questions from this project's own
  research files.

## Findings — a clean negative across every item checked

**Governing structural fact** (same pattern as `encyclopedia_weapons_wwii_1943.md`
found for the Encyclopedia of Weapons of WWII): every vehicle's spec block
(Weight/Crew/Motor/Speed/Range/Capacity/Length/Width/Height/Armament/
manufacturer/dates/quantity built) never includes an armor-thickness line,
for any vehicle, anywhere. Body-text captions occasionally mention armor in
passing but never a dimensioned plate/location breakdown:

- Panzer IV Ausf.G caption: "Note 30mm additional armor plate on driver's/
  radio-operator's frontal plate" — a general confirmation of the
  already-known appliqué-armor fact, not a new figure.
- Panzer III Ausf.M caption: "received the new armored plates in March 1943
  as additional protection against hollow charges and anti-tank rifles" —
  this describes Schürzen (spaced side skirts), not top/roof armor.

1. **Panzer III Ausf.M top armor** — clean negative. Spec box has no armor
   line; body text only discusses the March 1943 Schürzen addition.
2. **StuG III Ausf.G top/roof armor** — clean negative. Checked the 7.5cm
   StuG 40 Ausf.G "Abschlussausführung" and 10.5cm Sturmhaubitze 42 Ausf.G
   spec boxes specifically; no armor data of any kind.
3. **Panzer IV Ausf.H top armor / integral-vs-layered construction** — clean
   negative on both counts. No roof/deck figure, and the book doesn't
   discuss construction method at all (the Ausf.G caption's "30mm
   additional plate" wording is consistent with, not contradicting, this
   project's existing appliqué-vs-integral distinction, but adds nothing new).
4. **PaK 40 crew quality / 1943 fielding date** — not directly checked
   page-by-page (a soft negative, not exhaustive): this is a title-scoped
   AFV photo book ("Deutsche Panzer" = tanks), no towed anti-tank gun
   sections encountered in the sampled pages, and the book's spec-box format
   has no organizational/crew fields for any vehicle, making it structurally
   unlikely to help even if a PaK 40 section exists elsewhere in the book.

No new/citable findings turned up for Tiger or Panther in the pages sampled.

## Confidence Notes

- This is the second general-reference book this session (after the
  Encyclopedia of Weapons of WWII) to independently confirm the same
  structural pattern: a photo/spec compendium format simply does not carry
  plate-level armor data, regardless of how narrowly or broadly scoped the
  book's subject is. This strengthens the standing recommendation that only
  Panzer-Tracts-style technical references (not general histories or photo
  compendiums) are likely to resolve this project's remaining armor-geometry
  gaps.
- Image-scan-only, no text layer — read via direct page-image inspection,
  sampling rather than exhaustive page-by-page coverage. The PaK 40 item is
  flagged softer than the other three for this reason.

None newly opened. All four items checked remain exactly as open as before
