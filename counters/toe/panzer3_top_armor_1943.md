# Culver, *Panzer III in Action* — Does It Answer Panzer III's Top-Armor Question?

Research pass reading the full 48-page book at
`/home/spugnoid/dev/wdr-rules/reference/epdf.pub_panzer-iii.pdf`, specifically
to check whether it resolves this project's still-open Panzer III Ausf M
Top/Roof armor gap (`counters/toe/vehicle_top_armor_1943.md`'s Open Question
#1, which names *Panzer Tracts No. 3-3* as the ideal, not-yet-accessible
source).

## Short answer: this is not Panzer Tracts No. 3-3, and it has no armor data at all

**Title/author/publisher, confirmed from the book's own title page and
copyright page:** *Panzer III in Action* (Signal/Armor Number 1), by Bruce
Culver, Squadron/Signal Publications, copyright 1972, ISBN 0-89747-034-6.
This is **not** the Jentz & Doyle *Panzer Tracts No. 3-3* volume this
project's research has been looking for — a completely different publisher,
author, and era of book (Squadron/Signal's "In Action" series predates the
Panzer Tracts series by roughly two decades).

The book's own introduction states its scope explicitly, and it is worth
quoting in full because it directly explains why no armor data was found:
**"PANZER III IN ACTION is a new concept... There is no text to speak of,
since we feel there are enough publications available describing Panzers in
detail, but lacking the photo coverage that the discriminating collector and
model builder is seeking."** The entire 48 pages (all read directly, in full,
as images) are wartime photographs with short one-to-three-sentence captions
— vehicle markings, unit identification, tactical/anecdotal color, and general
mechanical description (engine, crew stations, maintenance). **No armor
thickness figure, of any kind, for any plate, on any Panzer III or StuG III
variant, appears anywhere in this book.** The final page (p.48/back cover) is
a color four-view camouflage-pattern illustration of "Panzerkampfwagen III
Ausf.M, 14.Panzer-Division, Ukraine, July 1943" — genuinely useful as a visual
reference for this exact variant's shape and camouflage pattern, including a
top-down view, but it is an artist's color plate with no thickness callouts
or technical dimensions of any kind.

## Findings by directive priority

1. **Panzer III Ausf M Hull Top/Bottom, Turret roof — NOT FOUND.** No figure
   anywhere in the book. This is the book's own stated scope limitation
   (photos and captions only, "no text to speak of"), not a search failure —
   the data was never going to be in a book explicitly built around avoiding
   technical text.
2. **Turret roof thickness — NOT FOUND**, same reason.
3. **Cross-check of the already-resolved Hull Front layered-plate
   construction (20mm applique + 50mm base, 56mm effective,
   `face_hardened_fraction=50/70`) — NOT CONTRADICTED, NOT CORROBORATED.**
   The book does describe face-hardened applique plates in general terms —
   p.12's caption states plainly, in the context of North Africa 1941-42:
   **"The new British six-pounder guns soon forced additional armor
   protection. Face hardened plates were bolted to both hull and turret."**
   This is consistent with, and a real independent photographic/textual
   confirmation of, the general phenomenon (bolt-on face-hardened applique
   armor being added to Panzer III in response to the British 6pdr) that
   this project's existing layered-plate treatment already models — but it
   gives no thickness figures for either the applique or the base plate, so
   it cannot confirm or refine the existing 20mm/50mm/56mm-effective numbers
   specifically for the Ausf M glacis. Recorded as circumstantial narrative
   support, not a numeric source.
4. **Other armor data (Hull Side/Rear, Turret Front/Side/Rear) — NOT
   FOUND.** No thickness figures for any plate anywhere in the book, per the
   same scope limitation.
5. **Is this book, or does it cite, *Panzer Tracts No. 3-3*? NO.** No
   bibliography, source list, or citation of any kind appears anywhere in
   this book — consistent with its own stated "no text to speak of" premise.
   It does not mention Panzer Tracts at all (unsurprising, since *Panzer
   Tracts No. 3-3* was published decades after this 1972 book). *Panzer
   Tracts No. 3-3* remains genuinely inaccessible and is still the best-named
   lead for this specific gap.

## A genuine, real, and interesting non-armor finding worth recording anyway

p.40's caption names a specific, dated, sourced combat anecdote that could be
useful for a future Rule 18.12-style entry if this project ever wants one for
a StuG III/Panzer III context: **"Hauptmann Peter Frantz... received the oak
cluster to the Knights Cross of the Iron Cross, after his outfit had destroyed
43 Russian T-34s in one defensive action near Stanowoje, March 14, 1943."**
This is a real, dated (14 March 1943), named-officer, quantified anecdote from
the "Grossdeutschland" division's StuG III unit — not investigated further or
independently corroborated this pass, and not directly usable without knowing
which specific gun/vehicle type was involved and at what range, but flagged as
a real lead for whoever next builds a StuG III historical-matchup entry.

## Confidence Notes

- **High confidence** that this book contains no armor-thickness data of any
  kind: the entire 48 pages were read directly as images, not sampled, and the
  book's own introduction explicitly states this is deliberate editorial scope,
  not an oversight on this research pass's part.
- **High confidence** this is not, and does not cite, *Panzer Tracts No.
  3-3* — confirmed by title page, copyright page, and the complete absence of
  any citation apparatus in the book.
- **Moderate confidence** in the p.12 face-hardened-applique caption as a
  real, independent corroboration of the *general phenomenon* already modeled
  for Panzer III's hull front — but it supplies no thickness numbers, so it
  cannot upgrade or refine the existing figures' confidence tier.
- **Low-moderate confidence** in the Peter Frantz/Stanowoje anecdote as
  presented — a single wartime propaganda-photo-caption source, not
  independently cross-checked this pass, but specific and dated enough to be
  worth a future verification attempt.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

1. *Panzer Tracts No. 3-3* (Jentz & Doyle) remains the single best-named,
   still-inaccessible source for Panzer III Ausf M's Top/Roof armor — this
   pass did not change that. This project's Panzer III top-armor gap remains
   exactly as open as before, now with one more source (a plausible-sounding
   but ultimately wrong candidate) ruled out.
2. The Peter Frantz/Stanowoje (14 March 1943) anecdote is a real, specific,
   dated lead for a future StuG III Rule 18.12 entry, not yet investigated
   for the specific gun/range/target-type detail a matchup entry would need.
3. Given this project's own reference library also contains a *different*
   general-audience/hobby-tier book explicitly about StuG III (the "Osprey
   New Vanguard 019, Sturmgeschütz III Assault Gun 1940-42" title, not yet
   read as of this pass) — that book covers the 1940-42 period specifically,
   before the Ausf G's 1943 configuration this project's roster models, so
   it is unlikely to resolve the Ausf G-specific Top/Roof question either,
   but has not been checked directly and should not be assumed empty without
   a look.
