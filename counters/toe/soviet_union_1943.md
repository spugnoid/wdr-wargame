# Soviet Union — 1943 Infantry Table of Organization and Equipment

## Sources

## Rifle Squad

The basic Red Army rifle squad (*otdeleniye*) was **9 men** under the *shtat* in force for most of 1943 (this squad size dates to the July 1942 reorganization and continued through the December 1942 *shtat* 04/551 and its 1943 amendments).

Within each 4-squad rifle platoon, two of the four squads carried one light machine gun and two carried a second, giving 6 LMGs per platoon rather than a flat "1 per squad" rule (see Platoon Organization). The two squad patterns, as amended in May 1943 (the point at which squad and platoon leaders were rearmed with submachine guns — see below), were:

**Squad pattern A (2 of 4 squads in a platoon) — 9 men:**
- 1x Squad leader (Sergeant) — PPSh-41 submachine gun
- 1x Light machine gunner (Junior Sergeant) — DP-28 light machine gun
- 1x Assistant gunner — rifle
- 6x Riflemen — 5 rifles, 1 PPSh-41 submachine gun

Totals: 1 DP-28, 2 PPSh-41, 6 rifles.

**Squad pattern B (2 of 4 squads in a platoon) — 9 men:**
- 1x Squad leader (Sergeant) — PPSh-41 submachine gun
- 2x Light machine gunners (Junior Sergeant each) — 2x DP-28 light machine gun
- 4x Riflemen — 3 rifles, 1 PPSh-41 submachine gun

Totals: 2 DP-28, 2 PPSh-41, 5 rifles.

**Squad leader's personal weapon:** Under the December 1942 *shtat* as originally issued, the squad leader (a Sergeant) carried a rifle. A May 1943 amendment rearmed all squad leaders (and the platoon commander) with the PPSh-41 submachine gun — this is the state of affairs by the July 1943 timeframe this document targets.

**Light machine gun (DP-28):** The DP-28 was organic **to the squad**, not held back at platoon level — every squad had at least one designated "machine gunner" post, and half the squads in each platoon had two. It was not pooled into a separate platoon machine-gun element; the platoon had no separate LMG section. (Contrast the *company's* Maxim heavy machine gun, which was a separate squad — see Company Organization.)

**Rifle type:** The *shtat* formally specified the SVT-40 self-loading rifle as the standard individual weapon for riflemen (with the Mosin-Nagant M1891/30 as the fallback/predecessor). In practice, production and reliability problems meant the bolt-action Mosin-Nagant remained the predominant rifle in Red Army rifle squads throughout the war, including 1943, despite the SVT-40 being the "paper" standard. Sources agree there is no reliable way to establish an exact Mosin-vs-SVT ratio within a squad for any given month of 1943.

## Other Squad-Level Units

**No distinct "Guards Rifle Squad" TO&E is documented for 1943.** The primary-source *shtat* evidence (04/501, the December 1942 Guards Rifle Regiment table, and its 1943 amendments) shows the Guards designation producing organizational differences at the **company and above** — a Guards rifle company had a two-gun Maxim machine-gun *platoon* rather than the standard company's single-Maxim machine-gun *squad* (see Company Organization), and Guards regiments carried an extra submachine-gun company and heavier mortar/machine-gun scales at battalion and regimental level — but the Guards rifle *squad and platoon* themselves followed the same 9-man / 4-squad structure, weapon mix, and same-May-1943 SMG rearmament as a standard line rifle regiment's rifle company. This is a meaningful finding for this project: if the game's existing data model assumes a materially different Guards rifle *squad* loadout (e.g. a flat 4x PPSh-41 allocation), that assumption is not supported by the sources checked here — see Open Questions.

**Submachine-gun-heavy squads did exist at this echelon, but as part of a separate, non-organic unit.** Since the December 1941 *shtat*, each standard Rifle Regiment (not each rifle company) included an all-SMG **Submachine Gun Company** of about 100 men, held at the regiment commander's disposal as a shock/assault unit rather than being part of any rifle company's own establishment. It was built from 3 platoons of 3 squads each, 10 men per squad, with every man armed with a PPSh-41 (or predecessor PPD). Guards regiments were authorized a *second* such company from March 1942 onward. This unit is one echelon "outside" a rifle company's own organization chart (it was a regimental asset that could be attached down), so it is included here for completeness on the "SMG-heavy squad" question, but it is not part of a rifle company's organic structure.

**The more directly relevant SMG-heavy unit inside the rifle company** is the **Submachine Gun Platoon**, discussed under Platoon Organization below — from mid/late 1943 one of the three rifle platoons in every rifle company (Guards and standard alike) was converted wholesale to submachine guns while retaining its organic light machine guns and sniper rifles.

## Platoon Organization

A standard Rifle Platoon (*vzvod*) had **4 rifle squads** (this had been standardized since the March 1942 *shtat*; earlier 1941 tables had used 2-4 squads of varying size).

**Platoon HQ** (December 1942 *shtat*, as amended May 1943) — 4 men:
- 1x Platoon commander (Lieutenant) — pistol and/or PPSh-41 (sources disagree on whether the May 1943 order gave him the SMG in addition to, or instead of, his pistol)
- 1x Deputy platoon commander (Senior Sergeant) — rifle
- 2x Snipers — rifles (sniper-variant, scoped)

**Full platoon totals (Rifle Platoon, mixed rifle/SMG loadout, as of the May 1943 amendment):** 40 men total; 25 rifles, 9 PPSh-41 submachine guns, 6 DP-28 light machine guns, 1 pistol. The 6 LMGs sit entirely within the 4 rifle squads (2 squads x 1 gun, 2 squads x 2 guns — see Rifle Squad above); the platoon HQ itself held no automatic weapon beyond the commander's sidearm/SMG.

**The Submachine Gun Platoon (mid/late 1943 conversion):** In the late summer of 1943, one of the three rifle platoons in every rifle company (per Kennedy's shtat-based account) or, per the independent Tacticalnotebook account of a May 1943 order, one of the platoons in the smallest ("bare-bones," 90-man) company establishment, was converted entirely to submachine guns. It kept the identical 4-squad structure, the same 6 organic DP-28 light machine guns, and both sniper rifles — only the riflemen's individual weapon changed from rifle to PPSh-41. Full totals: 40 men, 32 PPSh-41, 6 DP-28, 2 rifles (retained by the snipers), 1 pistol. The other two rifle platoons in the company kept their mixed loadout above, with only their squad leaders and platoon commander individually rearmed with SMGs.

This is one of the most load-bearing findings of this research: **by the July 1943 baseline the task specifies, every rifle company had one all-SMG platoon and two mixed rifle/SMG platoons, not three uniform rifle platoons.** Both sources checked here (Kennedy's shtat-derived account and the independent Tacticalnotebook piece) agree on this point.

## Company Organization

A full-strength Rifle Company (*rota*) under *shtat* 04/551 (December 1942), as amended through 1943, numbered **141 all ranks** — this figure appears independently in both Kennedy's shtat-based account and the Tacticalnotebook piece, which is good corroboration.

**Company organization, 141-man establishment:**
- **3x Rifle Platoons**, 40 men each (see Platoon Organization) — one converted to an all-SMG platoon by mid/late 1943, the other two mixed rifle/SMG.
- **Company Headquarters** — as of a May 1943 order, reduced to just 3 men: the Company commander (Captain), the company *Starshina* (senior NCO responsible for administration/discipline/supply), and a Sergeant company clerk. (The posts of Deputy Company Commander and Deputy Company Commander for Political Affairs, both present since 1941-42, were eliminated by this order.) Earlier-war versions of the company HQ (1941-42) additionally carried two dedicated snipers directly on the HQ establishment; whether these persisted alongside the simplified May 1943 HQ is not fully clear from the sources checked (see Open Questions).
- **Medical Squad** — 5 men (an NCO from the Medical Service plus 4 nursing orderlies).
- **Mortar Platoon** — approximately 9-10 men, with **2x 50-mm mortars** (the PM-38/PM-41 type, max range roughly 800m). This platoon had carried 3 tubes under the July 1942 *shtat*; the December 1942 *shtat* took one mortar squad away, using its horse-and-cart transport to instead move the new company Machine-Gun Squad's weapon (see below).
- **Machine-Gun Squad** — approximately 5 men, **1x Maxim M1910 heavy machine gun** (water-cooled, 7.62mm; by 1943 increasingly supplemented, not replaced, by the lighter air-cooled SG-43). This squad was newly added to the rifle company's own establishment by the December 1942 *shtat* — earlier in the war the rifle company's machine-gun element (if any) had been removed and consolidated at battalion level.

**Anti-tank rifles (PTRD/PTRS) were NOT organic to the rifle company.** They were held at the **battalion** level in a dedicated Anti-Tank Rifle Platoon (from the July 1942 *shtat* onward): 9 weapons organized as 3 squads, each a Sergeant plus 2-3 two-man gun teams. An earlier (March 1942-only) organization had briefly fielded a full Anti-Tank Rifle *Company* of 16 weapons in 2 platoons at battalion level, but this was cut back to the single 9-weapon platoon from July 1942 onward and stayed that way through the December 1942 *shtat*. This confirms the task's framing correctly excludes PTRD/PTRS from company-and-below scope for the standard rifle company — they sit one echelon up, at battalion.

**Guards company variation:** A Guards rifle company (shtat 04/501, December 1942) differed from the standard company chiefly in its heavy-weapons scale: instead of the standard company's single-Maxim Machine-Gun Squad, a Guards rifle company fielded a full **Machine-Gun Platoon of 2x Maxim M1910s**. Guards company HQ is also explicitly noted (in the source) as carrying 2 snipers. Guards rifle platoons and squads otherwise matched the standard organization described above, including the same mid/late-1943 SMG-platoon conversion.

**Historical context — reduced-strength companies:** Because Red Army rifle divisions routinely fell well below authorized "paper" strength, official and semi-official "reduced strength" tables existed from 1943 onward and were applied unit-by-unit as divisional manpower fell past defined thresholds (these were not standardized army-wide — different Fronts used different threshold/reduction schemes). One documented mid-1943 progression for a rifle company under shtat 04/551 (with 1943 amendments) went: 141 men (full) -> 111 men (drop the Machine-Gun Squad, cut each 9-man squad to 7 men while keeping one LMG per squad) -> 90 men (additionally drop the fourth squad from each of the three platoons, leaving 3 squads/platoon). This is useful background on how the Red Army modeled combat attrition organizationally, though it is a distinct mechanism from a simple "reduced/rear face" abstraction and is not itself part of the "full strength" TO&E requested by this task.

## Confidence Notes

- **Squad size of 9 men, 4 squads per platoon, 40-man platoon, 141-man company:** Well-established. These figures appear independently in both Kennedy's shtat-derived account and the Tacticalnotebook piece, which draw on different underlying document sets.
- **The mid/late-1943 conversion of one rifle platoon per company to an all-SMG platoon, retaining organic LMGs and snipers:** Well-established, corroborated by both sources, though they differ slightly on exact dating (Kennedy: "mid-1943" / "late summer 1943"; Tacticalnotebook: a specific May 1943 order). Both agree on the mechanism and result.
- **Uneven LMG distribution within a platoon (2 squads with 1 DP-28, 2 squads with 2 DP-28, i.e. 6 per platoon rather than a flat 1-per-squad rule):** Single-source (Kennedy's shtat table transcription for 04/551/amended). This is a specific, somewhat counter-intuitive detail; treat it as the best-supported figure available but not independently cross-checked against a second primary-document transcription.
- **Squad and platoon leaders individually rearmed with PPSh-41 by a May 1943 order, ahead of/alongside the full platoon-level SMG conversion:** Single-source (Kennedy), with an explicit note in that source itself that it is unclear whether the platoon commander retained his pistol in addition to, or in place of, the new SMG.
- **Maxim M1910 HMG Squad newly added to the rifle company (not battalion) under the December 1942 shtat, at 1 gun; Guards companies instead fielding a 2-gun Machine-Gun Platoon:** Single-source (Kennedy), but internally consistent with the same source's Guards annex and battalion-level rollups.
- **PTRD/PTRS anti-tank rifles held at battalion, not company, level (9 weapons in a single platoon from July 1942 onward):** Well-established — corroborated by a general web search on Red Army anti-tank rifle organization independent of Kennedy's paper.
- **No distinct "Guards Rifle Squad" TO&E in 1943 (Guards differences are at company level and above, not at squad/platoon level):** This is a negative finding — an absence of evidence for a distinct Guards squad organization in the sources checked, not positive proof that no such variant ever existed in any Guards formation's local practice. Treat as best-supported-by-available-sources rather than definitively closed.
- **SVT-40 as the "paper" standard rifle vs. Mosin-Nagant bolt-action rifles predominating in practice:** Well-established as a general historical point (widely stated across multiple sources), but no source gives a reliable numeric split for any specific month of 1943.

## Open Questions / Gaps for Follow-up

- **No distinct "July 1943" shtat number was found.** The task brief anticipated a specific "July 1943 reorganization/shtat," but the most authoritative source located (Kennedy, drawing on primary shtat documents) identifies December 1942's *shtat* 04/551 as the last full Rifle Regiment table issued, with the SMG-platoon conversion and squad/platoon-leader rearmament arriving as *subsidiary amendments* during 1943 (the Tacticalnotebook piece dates the company-establishment order specifically to May 1943) rather than as a wholly new numbered shtat. It's possible a genuine July 1943-dated shtat exists in the Russian-language archives (pamyat-naroda.ru) that neither secondary source used here happened to cite — this would be worth a follow-up search specifically for a "04/" numbered document dated mid-1943.
- **Whether the two company-level snipers, present on the Company HQ establishment since 1941-42, survived the May 1943 simplification of Company HQ to 3 men** is unclear from the source; it states the HQ was reduced to "just a Captain, Starshina and the Sergeant clerk" without explicitly saying what happened to the two dedicated snipers.
- **The miniatures.de wargaming TOE page for "Soviet Rifle and Motor Rifle Company, 1943"** gives a substantially different picture (a 3-Maxim machine-gun *platoon* built into every company, only 3 DP-28s company-wide, an anti-tank rifleman posted inside company HQ) with no sources cited. This was not used for any figure above, but its existence as a commonly-circulated wargaming reference that disagrees with the shtat-based sources is worth flagging — if this project encounters other data built from that page or similar uncited wargaming TOE charts, expect conflicts with the sourced figures here.
- **The project's existing preliminary data** (`counters/infantry_calc/data/units.csv`, rows `SOV_GDSRIF_1943.3_F`/`_R`) currently models a "Guards Rifle Squad" as a 9-man squad with 1x DP-28 and 4x PPSh-41, sourced only to "STAVKA TO&E 1943" with `PRELIMINARY` verify status. The research here did not find support for a Guards rifle squad with that specific loadout (4x SMG is much heavier than the 1-2x SMG per squad found in the shtat-derived sources, Guards or standard); this is flagged for whoever revisits that CSV row, not corrected here since this document's scope is research only.
- **David Glantz's major English-language works were searched but did not surface citable squad/platoon-level TO&E figures** in the search results available (his books are more focused on operational/strategic narrative and order-of-battle at division level and above). If a future pass has access to Glantz's *Companion to Colossus Reborn* (which appears to include statistical annexes) or Sharp's *Soviet Infantry Tactics in World War II* directly (rather than via secondary citation), it would be worth a direct check against the figures above.
- **Exact composition of "Deputy commander" weapon and whether the platoon commander carried a pistol, an SMG, or both** after the May 1943 rearmament order is explicitly noted as ambiguous in the primary source itself (documents reportedly show both).
